You kick off a long task in Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor. It's going to churn for forty minutes: reading files, running tests, fixing what breaks. You'd like a coffee.

You have two bad options:

- **Leave the Mac open.** One curious colleague, cat, or toddler, and a stray keystroke lands in the agent's prompt, or a click closes the terminal.
- **Lock the screen.** Now nobody can see the work, the display may sleep, and when you get back you have to reconstruct what happened.

## Botsitting

We call the middle ground **botsitting**: the agent keeps working *in full view*, and nobody can touch it.

That's what Wardlume does. Press **⌘⇧L** and a glass ward rises over your live desktop. The screen stays readable, so anyone nearby can watch the agent work. Keyboard, mouse, trackpad, and gestures are locked at the macOS event-tap level. Your Mac stays awake so the ward (and your agent) holds.

When you're back, rest a finger on Touch ID, or double-press your Apple Watch, and you're exactly where you left off.

## And if someone tries anyway

A wrong touch springs a reaction: a stern **ACCESS DENIED**, a glowering wizard, or a grumpy old man, with sound if you like. Or drop in your own image. It's surprisingly effective at coffee shops.

## Safe by design

- There's always a way out: Touch ID, Apple Watch, your password, or **⌘⌥Esc** Force Quit (which macOS reserves).
- Unplugging a monitor never unlocks it.
- It's local-only: no servers, no analytics, no accounts.
